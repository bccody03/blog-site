Opportunity This is the most under-exploited page on the site

Every other page competes for something crowded — essays about discipline, a book about relationships, a personal brand. This one offers free, one-to-one, personal replies from a real human being, and the supply of that is genuinely thin.

It currently targets none of it. The title, the H1 and the URL all say "Reflect with me" — a phrase you coined, which means the only people who can find it by name are people who already know you. Everything in section B below is about closing that gap.

I am not going to give you a search volume — I have no keyword data and inventing one would be worse than useless. What I can say is that the intent exists and the page is currently invisible to it. Check Search Console's Performance report filtered to /reflect.html: if it shows only brand queries, or nothing at all, that is this finding measured.

A. Technical

T29 Not one <label> on a page that is entirely a form High

Now — measured accessible names 
<label> elements on the page:  0

  #reflect-answer   required  →  accessible name: PLACEHOLDER ONLY
  #reflect-name     optional  →  accessible name: PLACEHOLDER ONLY
  #reflect-email    required  →  accessible name: PLACEHOLDER ONLY
Why it matters

Placeholders are not labels. They vanish the moment someone types, so anyone who pauses mid-form loses the only description of the field. They are announced inconsistently across screen readers. And they fail two WCAG criteria outright — 3.3.2 (Labels or Instructions) and 1.3.1 (Info and Relationships).

On most pages that is a moderate accessibility issue. Here the form is the page: every other element exists to get someone to fill it in.

Fix — and you have already written the label

<p class="reflect-prompt">What's on your mind?</p> is sitting directly above the textarea doing a label's job without being one. Change the element:

<label class="reflect-prompt" for="reflect-answer">What's on your mind?</label>

<label class="sr-only" for="reflect-name">First name (optional)</label>
<label class="sr-only" for="reflect-email">Your email, so I can reply</label>

/* styles.css — if you do not already have it */
.sr-only { position:absolute; width:1px; height:1px; padding:0; margin:-1px;
           overflow:hidden; clip:rect(0 0 0 0); white-space:nowrap; border:0; }

Zero visual change. Keep the placeholders as hints — they are good ones — but they stop being the only label.

T27 No structured data — and this page can declare a real offer Medium

Now

Measured: 0 JSON-LD blocks. Site-wide: index.html 5 nodes · articles.html 2 nodes · book.html 0 (T23, open) · reflect.html 0.

Why this page is different from the book page

On book.html I warned against adding offers, because the book is unpublished and any price or ISBN would be fabricated. Here the opposite applies. You are offering a real service that really exists and really is free — so Service with a zero-price Offer is an accurate statement, not a claim you would have to defend.

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",

    { "@type": "Service",
      "@id": "https://blakecody.com/reflect.html#service",
      "name": "Reflect with me",
      "serviceType": "Personal reflection and coaching correspondence",
      "description": "Write in about anything on your mind — a question about your
                      life and where it is headed, something you are stuck on — and
                      get a personal written reply.",
      "provider": { "@id": "https://blakecody.com/#person" },
      "areaServed": "Worldwide",
      "availableChannel": {
        "@type": "ServiceChannel",
        "serviceUrl": "https://blakecody.com/reflect.html" },
      "offers": {
        "@type": "Offer",
        "price": "0",
        "priceCurrency": "USD",
        "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock" } },
    { "@type": "WebPage",
      "@id": "https://blakecody.com/reflect.html#webpage",
      "url": "https://blakecody.com/reflect.html",
      "isPartOf": { "@id": "https://blakecody.com/#website" },

      "itemListElement": [
        { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 1, "name": "Home",

  ]

</script>

One caution that leads straight into T28: "price": "0" is true today. The day it is not, this block is a false statement in machine-readable form, which is worse than a stale sentence on a page.

T28 "Free for now" appears in three places and nothing will catch it going stale Medium

Now
LocationText
<meta name="description">"… Free for now."
og:description / twitter:description"… Free for now."
.coaching-free eyebrow"Work with me · Free for now"

Your own copy says this is temporary — "while I build my coaching practice". So the change is not hypothetical, it is planned. When it happens, five separate places need editing, and the one that matters most (the schema) is the one nobody will remember.

Fix

Nothing clever is needed — just make the change findable. Add a comment above the schema block naming every location:

<!-- PRICING: "free" is asserted in 5 places on this page — meta description,
     og:description, twitter:description, the .coaching-free eyebrow, and
     offers.price below. If this ever becomes paid, update all five. -->

If you would rather it be structural, drop offers from the schema entirely. Service without an Offer is still valid and says nothing that can become untrue.

T30 The topic chips do not expose their selected state Low

Now — measured 
.cat × 4    type="button"    aria-pressed: null    role: null

Selection is carried by an .active class and a hidden input. Visually it works. To a screen reader, all four chips are identical before and after a choice, so a user has no way to confirm which topic they picked — or that picking did anything.

The container is already correct: role="group" with aria-label="Choose a topic" ✓. Only the buttons are missing state.

Fix 
<button type="button" class="cat" data-cat="Relationships"
        aria-pressed="false">Relationships</button>

// in the existing click handler in app.js
reflectCats.querySelectorAll(".cat").forEach((c) => {
  c.classList.remove("active");
  c.setAttribute("aria-pressed", "false");
});
if (!isActive) {
  chip.classList.add("active");
  chip.setAttribute("aria-pressed", "true");
  reflectCatInput.value = chip.dataset.cat;
}

T31 The success message is never announced Low

Now — measured 
#reflect-thanks   aria-live: null   role: null

On submit the form is hidden and the thanks block revealed. Sighted users see it and it scrolls into view. A screen reader user gets silence — the form they were filling in simply disappears, with no confirmation that anything was sent.

On a page where the whole interaction is "I told a stranger something personal, did it arrive?", that silence is a real cost.

Fix 
<div class="reflect-thanks" id="reflect-thanks" role="status" hidden>

One attribute. role="status" carries an implicit aria-live="polite", so the heading and message are read out when the block appears.

B. Content & text

C27 The title targets a phrase only you use High

Now 
<title>Reflect with me — Blake Cody</title>        28 chars of ~60

"Reflect with me" is a good name. It is also a phrase you invented, which means the only people who can find this page by searching for it are people who already know it exists — and those people are already on your site.

Meanwhile the page offers something people genuinely look for and rarely find: a real person who will read what they write and answer, at no cost.

Change to 
Free Coaching — Write to Me, I'll Reply Personally | Blake Cody   62 chars

Or, if "coaching" feels like more than you want to claim yet:

Ask Me Anything About Your Life — Free, Personal Reply | Blake Cody  66, trim to ~60

On honesty: "coaching" is accurate here — your own intro says "while I build my coaching practice" — so it is a description, not a stretch. Do not add "certified", "professional", or anything implying credentials you do not hold. The appeal of this page is precisely that it is a person rather than a practice, and overclaiming would trade the thing that makes it work for a word that would not help.

Keep "Reflect with me" as the nav label, the eyebrow and the brand for it. The <title> is not branding — it is the sentence a stranger reads in a results list.

Now 
<h1 class="coaching-title">Reflect with me</h1>

Fourth page in a row with this shape. The homepage and archive are fixed; the book page (C21) and this one are open.

Change to

Or shorter, if the type size fights you: Free, personal replies to whatever you're working through.

Either states the offer in the reader's language rather than yours, and both read naturally under the existing "Work with me · Free for now" eyebrow.

C29 166 words, and no evidence anyone has ever done this Medium

Now — measured 
static words in <body>:  166      rendered: 140

The intro is well written and honest. But the page asks a stranger to write something personal to someone they have never met, and then offers nothing to suggest that anyone has done it, that a reply actually arrives, or what a reply looks like.

Every other objection on this page is answered — cost (free), privacy (private), timing (a few days). The unanswered one is is this real?

Fix

One or two short anonymised exchanges. Not testimonials — an actual paraphrased question and the shape of what you wrote back:

<h2>What this usually looks like</h2>
<blockquote>Someone wrote in last month about staying in a relationship
they had already decided to leave…</blockquote>
<p>What I said back, roughly: …</p>

Get permission first, always, and anonymise properly — no names, no identifying detail, and ideally paraphrase rather than quote. The trust this page runs on is the same trust you would be spending, so it is worth being conservative. If you are not comfortable using anyone's message, write a hypothetical and label it as one.

Three things at once: it is the social proof the page lacks, it is 200–300 words of indexable content on exactly the topics you want to own, and it is the most persuasive thing you could put here. Target 350–450 static words.

The form asks for maximum candour — "Say whatever's on your mind. The more honest, the more I can help." — and collects free-text personal disclosure plus an email address.

The policy itself is good. I checked: privacy.html covers the Reflect form specifically, names Formspree, and explains what happens to a message. That was done properly in pass 1. It is just linked in the one place nobody looks at the moment they hesitate.

Fix

.reflect-note already sits directly under the submit button and already says the right thing. Add four words and a link:

It costs nothing, it makes work you have already done visible where it counts, and on a page asking for personal disclosure it is the difference between "trust me" and "here is why you can".

C32 Nowhere to go for the reader who is not ready to write Low

Same as C25 on the book page. Apart from the nav, footer and "← Home" eyebrow, the body links nowhere. Someone who reads the offer, is interested, but is not ready to type something personal to a stranger today has one option: leave.

Add a closing line linking to the essays — ideally by topic, matching the four chips. "Not ready to write? Start with the essays on relationships." That converts a hesitant visitor into a reader instead of a bounce, and gives this page its first internal links with descriptive anchor text.

C. Tracking & measurement

K18 You measure completions but not attempts Medium

Now 
track("reflect-submit-" + category, "Reflection sent")   ✓ implemented

The per-category breakdown is genuinely useful and was the right thing to build first. What is missing is everything before the submit.

Why it matters

This form asks for more than any other on the site — a personal disclosure and an email address, from someone who may have arrived two minutes ago. The abandonment rate is therefore the number that tells you whether the ask is too large, and you currently cannot see it. Ten submissions could be ten attempts or two hundred.

Second gap: chips clicked versus chips submitted. That difference tells you what people arrive wanting to talk about versus what they actually go through with — and those are rarely the same on a page like this.

Fix 
// 1. form start — fires once, on the first keystroke
document.getElementById("reflect-answer")
  ?.addEventListener("input", () => track("reflect-start", "Started writing"),
                     { once: true });

// 2. topic selection, in the existing chip click handler
track("reflect-topic-" + chip.dataset.cat, "Topic chosen");

reflect-start → reflect-submit-* is your funnel. If starts are high and submits are low, the email field is the most likely blocker and is worth testing as optional-with-a-note. If starts themselves are low, the problem is C27/C29 — nobody is finding the page or nobody believes it.

K19 Verified working — and it already answers a question worth asking Pass

  • reflect-submit-<category> fires on success, with the chosen topic ✓
  • entryContext() — landing page, referrer, query — is spread into the Formspree payload ✓

Worth pointing out because it is easy to miss: that second one means every reflection you receive already tells you where the person came from. "Do these arrive from the homepage Explore card, from the nav, or from search?" is answerable today, from the Formspree inbox alone, with no additional work. That is a question most sites cannot answer at all.

C33 What is already right here — protect it Keep

  • The form is well constructed. required on the message and email, name deliberately optional, correct autocomplete tokens on both, a real <button type="submit">, role="group" with aria-label="Choose a topic" on the chips, and a 6-row textarea that visually invites a real answer rather than a sentence.
  • The expectation-setting is honest and specific. "I read every one. I'll reply by email — usually within a few days." Specificity is what makes a generous promise believable, and most sites hedge exactly here.
  • The offer is genuinely distinctive. Free, personal, from a named human. Nothing in this report suggests changing it — only that far too few people can currently find it.
  • Privacy coverage exists and is real — privacy.html names this form and this processor. C31 is only about where it is linked from.
  • The topic chips are the taxonomy the archive still needs (C18, page 02). Four categories that reflect how you actually think about the subject, already built and already collecting data on which ones people pick.
