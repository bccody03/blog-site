Why this page matters more than its traffic suggests

An About page is rarely a big traffic source, and this one probably never will be. It matters for a different reason: it is the page Google uses to decide who Blake Cody is as an entity, and it is the page a reader opens after an essay lands, when they are deciding whether to trust the person who wrote it.

Both of those jobs are currently done by a page whose title, H1 and schema say nothing. Everything below is about that.

A. Technical

T32 No ProfilePage or Person schema on the page that is about the person High

Now — measured 
about.html   JSON-LD blocks: 0
index.html   declares Person (@id …/#person) with sameAs and image  ✓
og:type      "profile"     ✓ correct — and a good instinct
profile:first_name / profile:last_name / profile:username   absent

The homepage asserts who Blake Cody is. The page actually about Blake Cody asserts nothing. For entity resolution that is backwards — search engines look to the About page to corroborate what the rest of the site claims.

Fix 
<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@graph": [
    { "@type": "ProfilePage",
      "@id": "https://blakecody.com/about.html#webpage",
      "url": "https://blakecody.com/about.html",
      "isPartOf":   { "@id": "https://blakecody.com/#website" },
      "mainEntity": { "@id": "https://blakecody.com/#person" } },
    { "@type": "Person",
      "@id": "https://blakecody.com/#person",     // same id as the homepage —
      "name": "Blake Cody",                        // this corroborates, it does not duplicate
      "homeLocation": { "@type": "Place", "name": "Michigan, USA" },
      "knowsAbout": ["relationships", "personal growth", "intentional living"],
      "sameAs": [ /* see T33 — every profile you actually control */ ] },
    { "@type": "BreadcrumbList",
      "itemListElement": [
        { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 1, "name": "Home",
          "item": "https://blakecody.com/" },
        { "@type": "ListItem", "position": 2, "name": "About" } ] }
  ]
}
</script>

Plus the two OG profile properties you already half-declared:

<meta property="profile:first_name" content="Blake" />
<meta property="profile:last_name"  content="Cody" />

Note the @id is deliberately identical to the homepage's. Two nodes sharing an @id merge into one richer entity; two nodes with different ids describe two different people who happen to share a name.

T33 sameAs lists two profiles, and this is the page that should list them all Medium

Now — measured 
rel="me" links on this page: 3   Substack ×2, App Store — correctly marked ✓
Person.sameAs (homepage):    2   Substack, App Store

Marking those rel="me" was the right call and it is already done. The gap is breadth: two corroborating profiles is thin for entity consolidation, and "Blake Cody" is a common enough name that Google needs help telling you apart from everyone else with it.

Fix

List every profile you genuinely control, in the homepage Person.sameAs and as rel="me" links here: Instagram, X, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, Threads — and, once Aligned ships, Goodreads and an Amazon author page, which are unusually strong signals for an author entity.

Only include profiles you actually own. A sameAs pointing at an account you do not control is worse than an empty list: it either fails to corroborate or corroborates the wrong person.

I cannot see which accounts exist — this one needs you. It is ten minutes of copying URLs and it is the highest-value ten minutes available for entity SEO.

T34 A hardcoded age, in three places, that goes wrong on a birthday Medium

Now
LocationText
body copy"I'm Blake Cody — 22 years old, living in Michigan."
og:description"I'm Blake Cody — 22, from Michigan…"
twitter:description"I'm Blake Cody — 22, from Michigan…"

Same shape as T28 on the Reflect page ("Free for now"): a true statement with an expiry date and nothing to catch it. Search engines and social previews cache descriptions for a long time, so a stale age can outlive the edit by months.

Fix

Drop the number. "Living in Michigan" or "in my early twenties" carries the same signal — young, early, figuring it out — without needing maintenance. The age is not what makes this page persuasive; the honesty is, and that survives the edit.

If you want to keep it precise, put it in one place (the body copy) and leave it out of the two meta descriptions, which are the ones that go stale invisibly.

C35 The page leads with aspiration and buries the evidence Medium

Now — measured 
static words: 195

The copy is honest and likeable and I would not change its voice. But look at the order. It opens with what you want to be:

"One day I want to be an author and a coach for life and relationships…"

Meanwhile the things you have actually done are further down or below the fold:

  • You write publicly, consistently, under your own name.
  • You are writing a book with a real first chapter someone can read right now.
  • You shipped an iOS app. It is live on the App Store. That is a genuinely hard thing that most people who say "one day I want to…" never do — and it is sitting in a card below the fold under a small grey label reading "What I've built".
Why it matters

Aspiration stated before evidence reads as inexperience. The same sentence stated after evidence reads as humility. It is the identical claim doing opposite work depending on where it sits, and this page currently has it in the weaker position.

Fix

Reorder, do not rewrite. Lead with what you are doing — writing publicly, writing the book, shipping the app — then the paragraph about where it is heading. Bring "What I've built" above the subscribe block, or fold the app into the main copy where a first-time reader will see it. Target 350–450 static words.

C36 "This site mirrors my Substack" Medium

Now
"This site mirrors my Substack — subscribe there to get every piece in your inbox."

This is C4 — the strategic finding running through every report — stated in your own words, on your own About page. It tells a reader that this domain is a copy, and it tells a search engine the same thing. "Mirror" is a word with a specific and unhelpful meaning in this context.

Fix

Now, at minimum: drop "mirrors". "I write on Substack, and everything I publish shows up here too" says the same thing without describing your own site as a duplicate.

After C4: the sentence inverts, and that inversion is the point of the whole strategy — "Everything I write lives here. Subscribe and it comes to your inbox too." Same offer, and the domain stops volunteering that it is the secondary copy.

C37 No internal links into the writing Low

The body links to Substack (twice) and the App Store. It does not link to a single essay, to the archive, to the book page, or to Reflect — all of which are one click away in the nav but get no contextual link with real anchor text.

Someone reading this page is at peak curiosity about you. Give them somewhere to go: name two essays you would want a new reader to start with, and link them inline. That is also the descriptive-anchor internal linking the site has almost none of.

C. Tracking & measurement

K21 The newsletter signup is unmeasurable, everywhere on the site Medium

Raising it here because About is a likely search landing page for your name, and "read about him, then subscribed" is the exact journey you would most want to see.

Now

The subscribe block on this page — and on the homepage, the archive and Reflect — is <iframe class="sub-frame"> pointing at bccody.substack.com/embed. It is a cross-origin iframe, so nothing inside it is observable from your page: not a focus, not a keystroke, not a submit. Same-origin policy, no workaround.

So: you can measure clicks on links to Substack (K3, working well), but the signups that happen in the embedded form — your primary conversion — produce no event on your side at all. Substack's own dashboard shows the subscriber; nothing connects it to the page they were on.

Fix

This is the same change the UI audit proposed for a different reason (X3 in ui/changelog-01-index.pdf, where the point was that the iframe imports Substack's visual design into your page). Replace the embed with your own email field and button posting to Substack's subscribe endpoint. Then:

track("subscribe-submit-" + location.pathname.replace(/\W+/g, "-"),
      "Newsletter signup");

One change, two audits satisfied: the form finally looks like your site, and you can finally tell which page earns subscribers. Given that the newsletter is the thing this whole site exists to grow, not being able to measure it is the largest remaining blind spot in the tracking setup.
